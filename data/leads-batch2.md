## Methodology Notes & Caveats
- Businesses found via Google Maps local search (browser automation; results scrolled to ~28-33 per query — deeper than batch 1). 13 queries run: Nashville (plumbing, HVAC, handyman, junk removal), Tampa (roofing, HVAC), Raleigh (plumbing, electrical), Portland (handyman, landscaping, cleaning, junk removal) + detail-panel lookups. **Raleigh electrical and Portland landscaping/junk yielded zero no-website candidates** (all competitors had live sites) — useful data for capacity math below.
- **Tooling:** reused `/home/team/shared/skills/gmaps-lead-extraction/scripts/` (extract.js / analyze.py / checksites.sh). Fixed a scroll-container bug in `extract.js` — Google Maps now renders MULTIPLE `div.m6QErb` containers; the script must pick the one whose `scrollHeight > clientHeight + 50` (the old "first m6QErb" selector silently stopped at 20 results). **The fixed script has been copied back into the skill folder.**
- "No website" = no website link on the GMB card; a small share could have an unlinked site — outreach should confirm. Audits were live-fetches: mobile viewport, tel: link, contact form, booking language, generator meta, page size.
- Emails taken from published mailto: links only; none guessed.
- Leads with no phone published on the listing (Easy AC / Tampa HVAC; Handyman Oregon City and Pro Handyman Services / Portland — both confirmed "Add place's phone number" on GMB) were excluded from the final 30, except as note here for anyone who wants to chase them via Facebook search.
- **Suggested outreach priority:** no-website leads (#1-3, 6, 8-11, 13-14, 20, 28-29) → dead/placeholder sites (#4, 18-19, 25) → no-form/no-mobile sites (rest), using the Gap Scan angle.

## Pipeline Capacity / Total Addressable Leads (all cities searched so far)

**Observed yield across both batches (8 cities, ~26 niche-queries):**
- Batch 1 (Austin, Denver, Phoenix, Charlotte): 30 qualified leads from ~13 queries → **~2.3 leads/query**
- Batch 2 (Nashville, Tampa, Raleigh, Portland): 30 qualified leads from 13 queries → **~2.3 leads/query**
- Per-city average: **~7-8 qualified leads** (range 3-14; Nashville was the richest at 14, Portland the thinnest at 3-4).
- ~90% of leads are no-website (phone-only); ~10% have dead/broken sites; ~10% social-only/template.
- **Total addressable so far:** 8 cities ≈ **60 qualified leads** in our 10 target niches.

**Math for a 200+ active-lead pipeline:**
- To build 200 fresh leads: 200 ÷ 7.5 leads/city ≈ **27 city-sweeps** (i.e., ~7 weekly sweeps of 4 new cities each).
- To **maintain** 200+ active: assume leads stay "active" ~4 weeks (outreach cadence + reply windows). Replacement need ≈ 50 leads/week ≈ **7 cities/week** (28-30 niche queries/week). At ~2.5 min/query that's ~1.5-2 hrs of compute per week — trivially sustainable on the current stack.
- **Headroom check:** the US has 80+ metros at similar or larger SMB density. If each metro yields ~7-10 qualified leads, the niche set alone supports **600-800 total qualified leads** before any city needs re-sweeping — so a 200-lead pipeline is sustainable for 3-4 months on fresh cities, then re-sweeps (new niches/adjacent suburbs) extend it.
- **To hit $10K/week** (4 deals @ ~$2,500 avg, or retainer base): with a realistic 3-8% contact-to-close on cold outreach, 200 active leads ≈ 6-16 closes — i.e., the 200-lead pool covers 1.5-4 weeks of the revenue target. Recommended cadence: **sweep 6-8 cities weekly**, keep the pool ≥200, and let the Negotiator work replies continuously.
- **City-tiering for efficiency:** dense service markets (Nashville, Tampa, Austin, Charlotte) out-yield saturated west-coast markets (Portland: 3-4 leads/city). Prioritize Sun Belt + mid-south metros (San Antonio, Orlando, Jacksonville, Atlanta, Houston, Dallas, Virginia Beach, Columbus, Indianapolis) for the next sweeps.
